# Office Hours: CourseRelay

Generated 2026-09-09 · Pre-product startup diagnostic · **Decision: build the evidence desk, not the translation marketplace.**

## Six forcing questions

### 1. Demand reality
There is no customer evidence yet. Source-market reviews and YouTube dubbing adoption are proxies, not demand. Evidence that changes the decision: three course creators grant rights and one regional seller either prepays, commits minimum distribution, or produces qualified buyer deposits.

### 2. Painful status quo
A creator considering localization uses platform analytics, spreadsheets, freelancers, AI dubbing tools and an affiliate's intuition. They cannot see target-language saturation, model the split, track rights/update obligations, or distinguish enthusiastic interest from a viable launch. A bad bet burns roughly $8k–$20k and weeks of coordination; a rights mistake is worse.

### 3. Desperate specificity
The first customer is **the growth/operator lead at a $500k–$5M English-language course business with 1,000+ reviews and 30%+ international traffic but no Portuguese product**. They are rewarded for new revenue without distracting the instructor. A failed localization wastes a quarter's growth budget and creates brand/support risk.

### 4. Narrowest paid wedge
Sell a two-week **Brazilian Portuguese localization opportunity brief** for one proven course: rights checklist, competing offers, buyer-language interviews, localized landing page, deposit test, production quote, conservative payback model and go/no-go recommendation. Price hypothesis: $1,500. Only after a paid brief passes does CourseRelay offer the pilot.

### 5. Observation and validation
No user has been observed. The next assignment is to sit silently through five operators' current localization-decision workflow, then run one deposit test with a partner's real audience. Watch where they distrust the score, what rights evidence they request, and whether they change assumptions instead of merely admiring the dashboard.

### 6. Future-fit
Machine dubbing makes audio production cheaper, so a generic translation service becomes less valuable. CourseRelay becomes more essential only if it owns the harder cross-market operating layer: rights, evaluation, cultural adaptation, updates, regional distribution evidence and cohort outcomes.

## Premise challenge

1. The problem is not translation; it is **confidently deciding which creator/course/market deal deserves capital**.
2. Doing nothing is rational unless international traffic or partner demand already exists.
3. A two-sided marketplace is premature. A concierge evidence brief creates learning and revenue with no marketplace liquidity.

## Approaches considered

### A. Concierge brief — minimal viable
- **Effort:** S · **Risk:** Low
- One market, one course, manually researched, with a deposit test and deal model.
- Pros: fastest paid learning; validates both sides; avoids production risk.
- Cons: service-heavy; low initial margin; depends on manual seller interviews.

### B. Opportunity desk — selected prototype
- **Effort:** M · **Risk:** Medium
- Reusable ranking, shortlist and scenario model backed by a human rights/demand process.
- Pros: demonstrates differentiated workflow; captures comparable decisions; becomes internal operating system.
- Cons: scores can imply false precision; data integrations do not yet exist.

### C. Localization marketplace — ideal later architecture
- **Effort:** XL · **Risk:** High
- Creator rights, production vendors, regional sellers, payments and outcomes in one network.
- Pros: compounding data and distribution; potential take-rate economics.
- Cons: cold-start on two sides, high compliance/support burden, easy to overbuild.

**Recommendation:** sell A, operate it with B, and defer C until at least 10 successful pilots show repeatable demand.

## Success criteria and assignment

- 10 operator interviews; 5 observed workflows.
- 3 written rights approvals for a brief.
- 2 paid $1,500 briefs within 30 days.
- One localized landing-page test with 20+ deposits or a predefined 3% qualified conversion threshold.
- **Assignment:** contact 20 course businesses that meet the ICP and ask to observe their last localization decision, not to demo software.
